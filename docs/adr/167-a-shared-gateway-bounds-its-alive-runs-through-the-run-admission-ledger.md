# ADR-167 — A shared gateway bounds its alive runs: a `gw:<host>` subject in the run-admission ledger, admitted before start and held through the runner's redelivery

- **Status**: Accepted — the operator decided it on 2026-11-13 (run admission completed by a transit guard, over a per-call semaphore and over a place taken at the runner's claim; a subbot child inherits its live ancestor's place; a deferred launch runs the current bot when nothing started; observe first, then a guarded enforce; `max_wait` bounds the whole wait; the subject is the host alone in v1)
- **Date**: 2026-11-13
- **Deciders**: jo (the arbitrations), Claude (three designs, two code maps, the plan, the store slice), codex gpt-6-astra (the adversarial review of the plan, 14 findings)
- **Extends**: the per-key hard cap's ledger, `store.CredSlotStore` ([`pkg/store/credslot.go`](../../pkg/store/credslot.go), [`pkg/store/mongo/cred_slots.go`](../../pkg/store/mongo/cred_slots.go), #2643) — nothing of it is reversed; its rows gain a second kind of key.
- **Relates to**: [ADR-131](131-tenant-gateway-credential.md) (the team's gateway key, whose host is the subject) · [ADR-164](164-configurable-key-pools-attach-per-scope-with-a-fallback-strategy.md) (key pools route spend; they bound no shared host) · [ADR-155](155-reserved-capacity-is-a-durable-allocation.md) (the durable capacity ledger, designed and not built, which left the parking semantics of #2643 to that ticket; this record takes them up for a subject that is not a key)
- **Serves**: [#3254](https://github.com/SocialGouv/iterion/issues/3254)

## Context

On 2026-11-13, 13 teams launched 22 Revi reviews within about a minute. Each team held its own PLIAGE key, and every key pointed at one LiteLLM host (claw, `deepseek-v4.1-flash`): about 12 runs executed at once. The latency of one call went from about 50 s to 4–7 min. Three runs were killed by review-pr's `max_duration: "180m"`, because the budget clock counts wall time inside the run (`pkg/runtime/budget.go`) and slow calls consume it.

Nothing bounds the runs of one gateway:

- the per-key cap (`max_concurrent_runs`) counts by fingerprint, and a gateway key's fingerprint folds in its base URL and its secret (`secrets.APIKeyFingerprint`), so 13 team keys are 13 rows — a floor of 13 runs, above the ~12 that saturated the host;
- `max_concurrent_runs: 1` on every team key still caps at 13.

The goal is at most N alive runs per gateway, all teams together, the runs past N waiting visibly with no pod started and no budget spent. It must hold across server replicas, be configured in the database, have an off lever, and implement an existing seam (philosophy 2).

The per-key cap already ships the seam. `store.CredSlotStore` keeps one row per key; an admission is one conditional write, so of N concurrent admissions on K free places exactly K win, whatever replica each ran on; an admission adopts the alive runs the row does not name and evicts the holders whose run stopped spending; a refused launch parks `CRED_SLOT_WAIT` and the retry sweeper asks again for free.

**The admission alone does not hold the bound** — the review's main finding, verified in the code. The publisher is not the only road to execution:

- a NATS delivery of a `failed_resumable` run is executed as a resume (`pkg/queue/admission.go`), and the runner calls the engine without going back through any admission;
- the runner produces that status on each of its nak paths — interruption or drain, sandbox timeout and sandbox capacity, attachments, orphan adoption — and the server's orphan sweeper does too;
- during those windows the holder predicate counts only `queued` and `running` (`RunStatus.HoldsCredentialSlot`), so the ledger judges the holder stale, evicts it and gives its place away, and when the redelivery lands the run resumes anyway.

With N = 1, two runs execute. The hole exists today for the per-key cap.

## Decision

1. **A gateway is a capacity subject of the run-admission ledger.** Its subject is `gw:<host>` (`store.GatewayCapacitySubject`): the lower-cased host the gateway client dials (`compatgw.Config.Host`), with no scheme, port or path. The `cred_slots` ledger, on both store twins, keys a row by a credential's fingerprint or by a capacity subject; the namespaces cannot collide, since a fingerprint is hex or `account:`-prefixed. Holding, adoption and eviction read one predicate for both (`Run.SpendsCredential`), and the Mongo filter mirrors it (`spendsCredentialFilter`, a `$or` over the two stamps).

2. **Every run that seals a gateway key is stamped, bound or not.** `Run.CapacitySubjects` (`capacity_subjects`) rides `RunCredStamp` beside `CredFingerprints`: written as one unit at launch, and re-stamped at every resume from the re-sealed bundle — both channels, `APIKeys` and `PinnedAPIKeys` — never from `ContentBoundary.GatewayHost`, which is frozen at launch. It is never folded into `CredFingerprints`, which the run snapshot, a usage wait's wall credentials and the credential walk read as the identities of credentials. Stamping without a bound is what lets a bound set during a burst count the runs already on the host (adoption), and it feeds a per-host gauge with no configuration. A resume's re-stamp fails closed as soon as its old or its new stamp carries a subject.

3. **Park before start.** The publisher reserves `gw:<host>` after sealing, before it writes or publishes the run, in the all-or-nothing loop that reserves the key slots — keys first, then the gateway, so a reservation given back on a key's refusal never touches the row every team shares. Refused, the run is parked `failed_resumable` with its retry armed in the same write: code `GATEWAY_SLOT_WAIT`, reason `gateway_slot`, a free re-ask at the entry's `probe_interval` (2 min by default). The budget clock has not started and `Restore` excludes stopped time, so the wait costs no pod and no budget. The refusal is a `CapacityRefusal{Kind, Subject, Label, Held, Max}`; for a gateway it answers `errors.Is(ErrGatewayAtCapacity)` and unwraps to `ErrCredentialAtCapacity`, so the retry sweeper, the 429 and the missions read it as they read a key's. The parked text names the host and the count across all teams, and no other team's run.

4. **The transit guard: the bound holds outside the admission.** Three closures, shipped before any `enforce`; they close the per-key cap's holes too.
   - **C1 — a continuation keeps its place.** One predicate, `Run.HoldsCapacity(now)`: `queued`, `running`, or `failed_resumable` with `continuation_state = redelivery_pending` and `updated_at` within `RedeliveryHoldTTL` (10 min by default, lever `ITERION_REDELIVERY_HOLD_TTL`, which covers the longest nak plus the orphan-adoption floor). The holder predicate, the eviction, the adoption and the count read it, on both twins, for keys and subjects alike.
   - **C2 — the runner re-asserts before any direct resume.** A ledger primitive, `ReassertCredSlot(ctx, subject, runID)`: a conditional write that moves the run's entry to a new instant if the entry exists — the shape of the ledger's in-place re-admission, with no max and no configuration. Under the run lock, before the engine resumes a delivery that read `failed_resumable` or adopted a `running` orphan, the runner CASes the continuation to `redelivery_pending`, then re-asserts each stamped subject:
     - every one held → the run resumes;
     - one missing → no resume: a free retry is armed, the message is acked, and the run goes back through the publisher's admission;
     - a ledger error → a deferred nak, never a resume on a guess.

     The order is the guarantee: an eviction judged before the CAS misses the moved entry, one that fires between the CAS and the re-assertion makes the re-assertion fail, and one judged after the CAS sees a holder.
   - **C3 — the launch closes after its write.** The same primitive runs after `PersistLaunchRun` and before publication. An entry gone means the launch stalled past `CredSlotGrace` between its reservation and its write and was evicted: one fresh reservation is tried, and if it is refused the run is CASed `queued → failed_resumable`, parked and armed, with nothing published.

5. **A tree is one place.** A subbot child runs in its parent's runner, under its parent's message, and carries no stamp of its own. A child resumed after its human gate is granted under the place of its live ancestor that holds `gw:<host>`, with no new entry, so N parents holding N places while each waits on its child's human answer never deadlock on a 429. A child whose ancestors have all ended asks for its own place.

6. **A deferred launch runs the current bot when nothing started.** A parked run with no checkpoint, no `started_at` and no runner claim is re-resolved on the current bot when it is admitted, and its inputs are revalidated against the current workflow as a launch's are; the version it runs is recorded on the run. The hash check is not opposed to it, so a bot updated during the wait spends no attempt. A run with a checkpoint keeps the hash check.

7. **Configuration: a platform family, observed first, enforced behind a guard.**
   - **The family.** `platformcfg` family `gateway_capacity`, one entry per canonical host: `max_concurrent_runs` (`0` is no bound on every branch — no reservation, refusal, offer, yield or would-park count, the meaning `0` already has on a key), `mode` (`observe` or `enforce`), `probe_interval` (strictly positive), a note. Super-admin routes with CAS and audit, a status route (alive runs and bots per host, and the alive runs on the host whose subjects do not name it (no `gw:<host>` for that host; the host a run is on is the base-URL host of the gateway key its latest stamp sealed — never `content_boundary.gateway_host`, which is frozen at launch and replayed onto subbot lanes — and a subbot child is attributed to its stamped ancestor)), the CLI and the OpenAPI.
   - **The cascade intersects.** The platform row is always reserved, a team key's own cap tightens it, and nothing below the platform raises it — a team would otherwise raise a bound it shares with twelve others.
   - **`observe`.** The publisher reserves nothing; it counts `iterion_gateway_would_park_total{host}` when the alive runs reach the entry's max, and admits.
   - **The activation.** About a week in `observe` on the per-host gauge, then `enforce` at 8, lowered toward 6 if the latency of one call stays above about twice its nominal value. The `PUT` that sets `enforce` is refused (409, with the count) while alive runs on the host carry subjects that do not name it (counted as the status route counts them); `accept_unstamped: true` is the explicit, audited escape. Before it, every server replica runs the enforce release and every runner the transit-guard release, read from the deployed digests, not from a tag.
   - **The rollback.** The lever is `mode: observe`, or removing the entry, effective within the resolver's 30 s; parked runs are admitted unbounded at their next re-ask.
   - **Failures.** A configuration never read since the replica started, with a gateway key sealed: the run parks, "capacity unknown" — a webhook launch has nobody to click again. A configuration that fails after a first read: the last known value. A ledger error: the launch is refused, because a hard bound that opens on an incident is only advisory.

8. **`max_wait` bounds the whole wait.** The horizon `scheduled_at + max_wait` is checked before an offer, before the cheap pre-check and before the resume, without spending an attempt — the promise the retry sweeper's comment already makes.

9. **The host alone in v1.** One subject per host. The model joins it (`gw:<host>|<model>`) on its own trigger — other models of the host taking its places — once route resolution exposes the model of each call site.

## Considered options

- **A semaphore per provider call**, waited on before each call (`callAndAggregate`). It needs a Mongo registry with a TTL, an IPC to the sandbox where neither Mongo nor NATS is reachable, a budget clock suspended while waiting and a node deadline that moves (`engine_exec.go` freezes it at node start), and it fails open on a Mongo outage by default. It does not do better on the 2026-11-13 burst: every pod starts and every call queues. It is **kept as a later step**, built on any one of these triggers: a measured LLM duty cycle ≤ 0.5, where the admission immobilises more than half the capacity; a fan-out bot that saturates the gateway alone; significant server-side traffic on the bounded host (supervisors, the conflict agent, the session board); the need to give the place back during model-idle phases or while a parent waits on its child. Its prerequisites are an ADR on the suspended clock and the moving deadline, and an IPC version marker for the sandbox.
- **Key pools, or a cap on each team's key.** A cap of one on each of 13 keys still allows 13 runs, above the saturation point, and a pool routes spend between keys without bounding the host they share (ADR-164). A team key's cap stays in v1 as the per-team share inside the gateway's bound.
- **Taking the place at the runner's claim**, the publisher's admission kept as an early filter. One enforcement point that every execution crosses, future ones included — but the runner must then read `gateway_capacity` and the key caps, a refusal at claim acks and parks from the runner, and two acquirers have to be reconciled. Rejected for C1–C3, where the publisher stays the only acquirer and the runner only re-asserts a place, with no configuration. It wins if an execution path that bypasses the publisher appears.
- **Accepting "N plus the runs in transit"** and measuring, with no re-assertion in the runner. No runner change — but the bound gives way exactly during a deploy's drain, when redeliveries are the most numerous and the soft-order pass would hand the drained places to parked runs. It wins only if redeliveries prove rare and the margin under the knee comfortable.
- **Folding `gw:<host>` into `CredFingerprints`.** One field fewer, and every reader that takes a fingerprint for a credential's identity would lie.
- **A separate `gateway_slots` ledger.** A second admission, adoption and eviction to keep in step with the first, for the same conditional write.
- **A strict FIFO from the first enforced slice** — a revision counter on every ledger write, key rows included. It stays on its trigger: overtakes measured despite the soft order.

## Consequences

- The ledger becomes the run-admission ledger, for keys and gateways alike, and the transit guard closes the per-key cap's existing redelivery and stalled-launch holes.
- New durable state, each with its cloud twin in its slice: `capacity_subjects` on runs (both twins), `gw:` rows in `cred_slots`, the `gateway_capacity` platform document. The status index serves both arms of the holder filter; its cost under load is measured, not assumed.
- **Mixed versions.** An older publisher reserves keys only and an older runner resumes without re-asserting: hence the version checklist before `enforce`. A pre-T1 replica's `SaveRun` keeps `capacity_subjects` (fields unknown to its schema are preserved) and its `SetRunCredStamp` leaves them in place: a run whose re-resolution it stamps keeps its old subjects, so when the key moved off that gateway — to another one, or to a key on no gateway — the run is over-counted on its old host until a re-stamp by a T1-or-later replica rewrites its subjects, or it ends; and when it moved to another gateway it is under-counted there. A T1 replica's `SetRunCredStamp` writes the subjects of a stamp that carries none — it clears them — so a T2-stamped run it resumes during a T1→T2 rolling deploy loses its `gw:<host>` while it keeps running there: under-counted until its next re-stamp. Every under-count is a run alive on a host whose subjects do not name it, which is what the status route counts and what the activation guard refuses `enforce` on (§7); over-counts and under-counts alike bias the `observe` gauge.
- A long run with few calls holds its place for its whole life: N is sized in runs, not in calls, until the per-call step's trigger fires.
- The wait is visible: the existing `run_retry_scheduled` event gains `waits_on`, `held`, `max` and `position`; the studio reads "waiting for gateway capacity · 4th" with a chip that is not red; the pull request gets a pause notice of its own, with no time and no "quota", once per episode.
- The node deadline frozen at node start kills a slow run whatever the cause. That defect is a ticket of its own: the bound removes the 2026-11-13 cause, not it.

## Slices

Each slice owes `task check` (CI adds `race`, `vendor-check` and `mongo-conformance`), every test seen red under its named mutation, its Mongo protocol tests run on a real replica set (a local skip is not a pass), a review loop with its trailers, its documentation in the same change, and a sub-issue of #3254.

- **T0 — measure**, no code: that the 22 runs sealed an `openai_compatible` key and carry `content_boundary.gateway_host`; the host's latency against concurrency on 2026-11-13; review-pr's LLM duty cycle and concurrent calls per run; the other bots and models on the host; the subbot bots with a human gate; the naks and redeliveries per day and per deploy.
- **T1 — the ledger accepts subjects that are not keys.** Store only, no behaviour change: `Run.CapacitySubjects` and `RunCredStamp.CapacitySubjects`, both `SetRunCredStamp`s, the predicate's second arm and its Mongo `$or`, `GatewayCapacitySubject`, the ledger's documentation; the conformance rows `CapacitySubjectHolder`, `CapacitySubjectIsNotAFingerprint` and `CapacitySubjectStampRoundTrip`, on both twins.
- **T2 — the stamp and the gauge.** The subjects stamped at launch and at resume, the fail-closed re-stamp, the per-subject count, `iterion_gateway_alive_runs{host}`, the status route with its count of the runs alive on a host whose subjects do not name it (the host read from the run's latest sealed gateway key, subbot children attributed to their stamped ancestor).
- **T3 — the `gateway_capacity` family, in `observe`.** Validation and warnings, the Mongo and memory twins, `Resolver.Ready()`, the routes, the CLI and the OpenAPI, `would_park`, the operator documentation.
- **T4a — the transit guard.** C1, C2, C3 and the park armed in one write, proven on two Mongo clients.
- **T4b — `enforce`.** The gateway's reservation after the keys', `CapacityRefusal` and `ErrGatewayAtCapacity`, `GATEWAY_SLOT_WAIT`, the 429 `gateway_at_capacity`, "never read" parks, the tree and the deferred-launch defaults, the activation guard.
- **T5 — the wake and a soft order.** The parked run's wait identity on its retry state, an offer that respects a live claim, a capacity pass that resumes what it offers outside the due batch, a cheap pre-check, the yield to older parked runs when `max > 0`, the release of parked runs when an entry goes to `max: 0`, the whole-wait `max_wait`.
- **T6 — the wait made visible**, up to the pull request's notice.
- **Later, each on its trigger:** a platform per-team share of a gateway, the `gw:<host>|<model>` subject, a gateway funded by a pool's environment, the strict FIFO, the per-call semaphore.
